Institutions, Governance & Funding

My current research examines the changing role of cultural institutions and the systems through which artistic activity is funded, administered and legitimised. I am particularly interested in the consequences of the shift from individual and private forms of patronage towards increasingly bureaucratic models of cultural provision.

The work considers institutional incentives, governance, cultural administration, public and philanthropic funding, artistic autonomy and the relationship between cultural organisations and the state.

03

Lost Art & Institutional Memory

The UnSeen is a research project concerned with works that disappear from cultural life: works that are lost, neglected, inaccessible or no longer performed, and the institutional conditions that determine what survives.

This strand asks how institutions construct cultural memory, and what is lost when preservation becomes detached from the conditions of artistic production and renewal.

Patronage & Creative Agency

A second strand of my research examines patronage not simply as a source of finance but as an institutional relationship: one that distributes authority between artists, patrons, administrators and audiences.

This provides a bridge between my contemporary work on cultural funding and my historical research into the structures that governed operatic production.

05

Opera as an Institution

My doctoral research at Oxford examined the Paris Opéra during the Ancien Régime through its performers and institutional structures. Rather than treating singers principally as interpreters of works, I studied them as economic and creative actors operating within systems of casting, employment, law, administration and patronage.
