Museum of the Home:
legal change or managerial change?

This page tests two histories against each other: what managers said the Museum was becoming, and what the governing-document record can actually prove changed in law.

The chronology is already revealing.

By the 2017–18 reporting year, management was already describing the institution as having been re-defined as the “Museum of the Home” and presenting a manifesto centred on the meanings of home. That predates the verified September–October 2020 constitutional/object filings and predates the 2026 legal company-name change. The managerial conception therefore cannot simply be treated as a consequence of the 2026 constitution.

Institutional account · C1

2017–18: managerial conception

The audited annual report records management's claim that the Museum's purpose had been re-defined and reproduces its manifesto. Because this is management commentary inside an audited report, the financial statements are audited but the interpretation of institutional purpose is not thereby independently verified.

Constitutive record · A1/A3

2020: legal breakpoint

The Charity Commission records an amendment on 9 September 2020; Companies House records a statement of company objects filed on 6 October. This is presently the strongest verified breakpoint for investigating when the current legal objects entered the constitution.

Corporate filing · A1

2021: recoverable pre-2026 text

A 15-page Memorandum and Articles was filed in April 2021, followed by an alteration resolution in June. That text is the correct comparator for the 2026 Articles.

Corporate filing · A1

2026: constitution + legal identity

New Articles were adopted on 17 March; the legal company name changed in April. We do not infer from those events alone that the charitable objects changed.
