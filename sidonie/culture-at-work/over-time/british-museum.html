Culture at Work · Institution over time

British Museum:
the institutional genealogy.

This timeline follows changes that altered the Museum’s legal purpose, governance, access, public-management regime or practical accountability. It distinguishes a change in the institution from a change in the language used to describe it.

1753 statutory foundation→1963 modern statute→NDPB + exempt charity→DCMS sponsor / Principal Regulator→2025–28 framework
1753
Law / purpose

Foundation by Act of Parliament

Documented change

The British Museum is established by Act of Parliament around the Sloane, Cotton, Harley and Royal library collections.

Why it matters

Creates a national statutory institution and trustee structure.

What remained

The institution did not yet operate under the modern NDPB/charity/accountability vocabulary.

Evidence: R39;R40 · documented fact
1759
Access / institution

Museum opens at Montagu House

Documented change

The Museum opens to visitors. The Museum’s own history notes that early access still depended on tickets and limited hours.

Why it matters

Turns the statutory collection into a public-facing institution, but access was not yet equivalent to modern open admission.

What remained

The statutory foundation remained; practical access arrangements evolved over time.

Evidence: R40 · documented fact + institutional account
1830s onward
Access / management

Access progressively widened

Documented change

Opening regulations and hours are progressively liberalised.

Why it matters

The meaning of a 'public museum' changes in practice without requiring a wholly new institution.

What remained

Core trusteeship and collection functions persist.

Evidence: R40 · institutional account
1963
Law / governance

British Museum Act 1963

Documented change

The 1963 Act becomes the Museum’s principal modern statute and creates a separate body for the Natural History Museum.

Why it matters

Recasts the modern trustee/statutory architecture; section 6 requires the Director to be appointed by Trustees with Prime Ministerial approval.

What remained

Trustees remain responsible for safekeeping, care and public availability of the collection.

Evidence: R41;R42 · documented fact
2011
Law / regulation

Exempt-charity framework

Documented change

Under the Charities Act 2011 the Museum is an exempt charity; DCMS is its Principal Regulator.

Why it matters

Makes clear that charity-law duties coexist with public-body sponsorship rather than ordinary Charity Commission registration.

What remained

Trustees remain the governing body rather than DCMS becoming the Museum’s board.

Evidence: R39 · documented fact
2013
Public management

Cultural Freedoms status

Documented change

The Museum becomes part of the Cultural Freedoms regime, modifying specified operating and financial requirements.

Why it matters

Adds operational freedoms without removing the Museum from central-government classification or sponsor-department accountability.

What remained

Statutory duties and the public-finance relationship remain.

Evidence: R42 · documented fact
2022
Governance

Governance Principles updated

Documented change

The Board’s Governance Principles state how trustees intend to discharge statutory, charity-law and public-body responsibilities.

Why it matters

Makes the Museum’s internal governance rules more legible and explicitly incorporates Nolan/public-body standards.

What remained

The British Museum Act remains the legal foundation; internal governance documents do not replace it.

Evidence: R43 · documented fact
2023
Crisis / governance

Collection-security crisis disclosed

Documented change

On 16 August 2023 the Museum announces that collection items had been found stolen, missing or damaged and that police were investigating.

Why it matters

Turns collection security, documentation, trustee oversight and internal controls into central governance questions.

What remained

The Museum’s statutory duty to safeguard and care for the collection was not newly created by the crisis.

Evidence: R44 · documented fact
2024
People / governance

Nicholas Cullinan becomes Director

Documented change

Nicholas Cullinan takes office as Director in June 2024.

Why it matters

Changes executive leadership during the post-2023 governance and recovery period.

What remained

The Director remains responsible to Trustees; the statutory appointment architecture persists.

Evidence: R39 · documented fact
2024–25
Management / crisis

Security and governance remediation enters formal risk reporting

Documented change

The annual report identifies loss/theft and damage to the collection as risks and lists documentation/digitisation, new security procedures and a collection audit among mitigations.

Why it matters

Shows the 2023 crisis becoming embedded in formal risk-management and organisational-control processes.

What remained

The report is management’s own account of mitigation; effectiveness requires independent or subsequent evidence.

Evidence: R45 · institutional report / documented disclosure
2025
Governance / finance

New DCMS framework agreement

Documented change

DCMS and the Museum publish a formal framework agreed under Managing Public Money and approved by HM Treasury.

Why it matters

Spells out the present sponsor relationship: central-government classification, NDPB status, exempt-charity status, Principal Regulator, appointments, accountability and financial controls.

What remained

The framework expressly says it creates no new legal powers; statutory authority remains rooted in legislation.

Evidence: R42 · documented fact
2026
Governance

Framework updated

Documented change

The 2025–28 framework is updated, including clarification of support for the Treasure Valuation Committee through a Museum subsidiary.

Why it matters

Demonstrates that the administrative architecture itself is a living document that can change between statutory reforms.

What remained

The underlying British Museum Act and trustee duties remain.

Evidence: R42 · documented fact
