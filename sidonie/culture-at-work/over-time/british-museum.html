This research file begins with constitutive law, parliamentary records and formal administrative instruments. Museum-authored histories are not used here to establish substantive historical propositions where upstream evidence can be recovered.

Evidence rule: absence is visible. Where SIDONIE has not yet recovered the relevant founding deed, Treasury minute, parliamentary paper or archival record, the page says so rather than filling the gap from later institutional commentary.
1963
PRIMARY — STATUTE

Modern statutory settlement

Documented fact

The British Museum Act 1963 provides the modern statutory architecture, including separate Natural History trustees and statutory collection/repository provisions.

Why it matters

This is constitutive evidence: it tells us what Parliament made the institution legally responsible for, rather than how later managers describe it.

What remained / evidentiary limit

The present administrative framework remains downstream of the Act.

Current sponsor framework

Documented fact

The DCMS framework sets out the contemporary sponsor, finance, appointment and accountability relationship.

Why it matters

It is evidence of current administrative machinery, not the source of the Museum's underlying statutory powers.

What remained / evidentiary limit

The framework itself says it does not create legal powers/responsibilities.
