Culture at Work · Institution over time

National Portrait Gallery:
institutional genealogy.

From historical celebrity to a statutory national institution with an expanding account of portraiture.

1856
Foundation / purpose

National Portrait Gallery founded

Documented change

Founded to collect portraits of 'the most eminent persons in British history'; early trustees prioritised sitter significance over artistic merit.

Why it matters

Establishes an unusually explicit selection principle centred on historical eminence.

What remained

Portraiture serves historical understanding from the outset.

Evidence: NPG1;NPG2
1896
Estate / institution

Moves to St Martin's Place

Documented change

The Gallery acquires its purpose-built home beside the National Gallery.

Why it matters

Gives the institution a permanent national public presence.

What remained

Its historical/portrait purpose persists.

Evidence: NPG1
1992
Law / governance

Museums and Galleries Act

Documented change

The Act establishes the modern Board and statutory functions.

Why it matters

Codifies duties to maintain, preserve, add to and exhibit the collection and promote understanding of British history through portraiture.

What remained

The original concern with people in British history survives in broader statutory form.

Evidence: NPG3;NPG4
2023
Estate / strategy

Reopens after redevelopment

Documented change

The Gallery reopens following major capital redevelopment.

Why it matters

Changes the visitor environment and institutional presentation, creating a point for pre/post comparison.

What remained

The statutory collection and governance framework remain.

Evidence: NPG1
2026
Governance / finance

DCMS framework 2026–29

Documented change

A new framework spells out classification, appointments, financial controls and the DCMS relationship.

Why it matters

Board elects Chair; trustees are PM-appointed, with two ex-officio trustees and an Artist Trustee; Director requires PM approval.

What remained

The document creates no legal powers; the 1992 Act remains upstream.

Evidence: NPG4
