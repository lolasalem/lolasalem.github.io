Culture at Work · Institution over time

Tate:
institutional genealogy.

From a National Gallery annex to an autonomous statutory national collection.

1897
Institution

National Gallery of British Art opens

Documented change

The Millbank gallery opens under the National Gallery's wider governance and becomes known as Tate.

Why it matters

British art gains a dedicated home, but Tate is not yet the independent statutory body it later becomes.

What remained

The collection remains embedded in National Gallery governance.

Evidence: T1
1954–55
Governance

Separate Tate trustee structure

Documented change

Post-war legislation establishes a separate Tate Board while retaining a formal National Gallery link.

Why it matters

Institutional autonomy increases while an explicit governance bridge survives.

What remained

The national collections remain legally connected through board representation.

Evidence: T1;T2
1992
Law / governance

Museums and Galleries Act

Documented change

The Act establishes the modern Tate and National Gallery corporate Boards and functions.

Why it matters

Creates the current statutory basis and preserves reciprocal trustee appointment.

What remained

Arm's-length trusteeship and the cross-board relationship remain.

Evidence: T2;T3
2000
Estate / organisation

Tate Modern opens

Documented change

A major new London site opens for international modern and contemporary art.

Why it matters

Changes Tate's scale, reach and organisational complexity without rewriting core statutory governance.

What remained

National-collection responsibilities remain with Tate's Board.

Evidence: T1
2025
Governance / finance

DCMS framework 2025–28

Documented change

The framework specifies current ministerial, Board, appointment and spending relationships.

Why it matters

Makes PM appointments, Board chair selection, Director approval and National Gallery cross-appointment explicit.

What remained

The framework operates alongside the 1992 Act.

Evidence: T3
