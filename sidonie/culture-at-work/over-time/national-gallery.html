National Gallery:
source genealogy.

This research file begins with constitutive law, parliamentary records and formal administrative instruments. Museum-authored histories are not used here to establish substantive historical propositions where upstream evidence can be recovered.

Evidence rule: absence is visible. Where SIDONIE has not yet recovered the relevant founding deed, Treasury minute, parliamentary paper or archival record, the page says so rather than filling the gap from later institutional commentary.
1824
PRIMARY — PARLIAMENTARY RECORD

Treasury-controlled national collection

Documented fact

Parliamentary debate on the Angerstein purchase records that general control and superintendence would lie with the Lords of the Treasury.

Why it matters

This is contemporaneous evidence that public funding and Treasury authority were present at the Gallery's origin.

What remained / evidentiary limit

Later trustee/director arrangements changed the distribution of authority.

Source genealogy → U4
1855–56
PRIMARY — PARLIAMENTARY RECORD

Directorate and revised governance

Documented fact

Hansard records the Treasury Minute of 27 March 1855 creating a Director and specifying relations among Director, trustees, Treasury and parliamentary reporting.

Why it matters

This lets SIDONIE reconstruct an actual change in decision rights rather than relying on a later institutional summary.

What remained / evidentiary limit

The Gallery remained publicly funded and subject to parliamentary Supply.

Source genealogy → U5
1992
PRIMARY — STATUTE

Modern statutory settlement

Documented fact

The Museums and Galleries Act 1992 establishes the current Board/functions and Tate cross-appointment.

Why it matters

The modern governance architecture becomes statutory rather than resting only on Treasury administrative arrangements.

What remained / evidentiary limit

Public sponsorship remains upstream of the institution.

Source genealogy → U3
2026
PRIMARY — ADMINISTRATIVE

Current sponsor framework

Documented fact

The framework specifies present DCMS accountability, appointments and financial controls.

Why it matters

It reveals the modern administrative layer superimposed on the statutory Board.

What remained / evidentiary limit

The framework does not replace the 1992 Act.
