How SIDONIE weighs evidence

Not all citations carry the same evidentiary weight. SIDONIE begins with the least-mediated surviving evidence. Institutional self-description is evidence of what an institution says about itself, not a privileged account of institutional reality.

A1 · Highest priority

Primary — constitutive / statutory

Acts, Royal Charters, founding deeds and bequests, statutory instruments. Used for legal existence, powers, duties, Board constitution and statutory purpose.

A2 · Highest priority

Primary — parliamentary / government

Hansard, Supply and Estimates, Treasury minutes/directions and appointment instruments. Used for formal public authority, appropriations and contemporaneous government decisions.

A3 · Financial authority

Primary — audited finance

Audited accounts, NAO-certified accounts and company accounts. Accounting income is not automatically treated as spendable operating resource.

A4 · Administrative

Primary — framework / administrative

DCMS frameworks, delegation letters and grant agreements. Evidence of the contemporary administrative relationship; not a substitute for upstream law.

B1/B2 · Corroboration

Records & independent scrutiny

Board minutes, archives, FOI, courts, NAO, PAC, select committees, regulators and serious scholarship. Used to reconstruct decisions and test official accounts.

C1 · Treat cautiously

Institutional account

Museum histories, strategies, mission statements, press releases and management speeches. Important factual propositions should be corroborated upstream.

Conflict rule: where institutional commentary conflicts with statute, audited evidence, contemporaneous records or credible independent scrutiny, SIDONIE displays the discrepancy rather than silently reconciling it.
