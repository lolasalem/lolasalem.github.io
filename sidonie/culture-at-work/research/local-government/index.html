Devolution diagnostic

These fields turn claims about partnership, proximity or localism into questions answerable with documents and data.

Legal competence

What legal power or duty actually exists?
Evidence: Act / statutory instrument / governing instrument

Fiscal control

Who controls the money before and after devolution?
Evidence: Settlement / grant determination / budget

Ring-fencing

Can the money be moved to another priority?
Evidence: Grant conditions / assurance framework

Decision authority

Who makes the allocation or strategy decision?
Evidence: Constitution / scheme of delegation

Artistic judgement

Who assesses artistic quality and by what criteria?
Evidence: Assessment framework / panel terms

Appointment power

Who selects those decision-makers?
Evidence: Appointment record / board terms

Scrutiny / appeal

Who can challenge or scrutinise a decision?
Evidence: Scrutiny rules / complaints / appeal

Administrative capacity

What specialist cultural staff and institutions exist?
Evidence: Org chart / staffing / delivery record

Institutional depth

How long has the machinery existed?
Evidence: Historical records / annual reports

Recipient dependence

Evidence: Audited accounts / grant records

Asset ownership

Who owns venues, collections and property?
Evidence: Accounts / statutes / leases

Delivery model

Direct service, trust, charity, grant or contract?
Evidence: Constitution / accounts / contracts

The same word — devolution — can describe very different institutional realities. These records separate settlement, funding control, cultural allocation routes, decision authority, personnel and scrutiny.

Strategy → machinery → results

SIDONIE compares what institutions say they intend to do with the powers, money, personnel and decision processes actually available, then with observable allocations and outputs. The comparison is descriptive: a flagged tension is not a verdict on motives or policy success.

How SIDONIE describes discrepancies

These labels are evidence states, not political ratings. They distinguish different reasons why strategy and machinery may not line up.

Aligned

Documented machinery and observed outputs directly correspond to the stated objective; does not prove causal success.

Partial institutional alignment

Some machinery clearly supports the objective, but important powers, funding routes or decision processes remain elsewhere or unresolved.

Authority gap

The objective requires a power the responsible institution does not demonstrably hold.

Funding gap

Responsibility is assigned without identifiable resources adequate to the mechanism described.

Control gap

Money moves, but relevant decision authority remains elsewhere.

Capacity gap

Power/funding exists but specialist administrative capacity is not evidenced.

Judgement gap

Allocation exists but who exercises artistic/cultural judgement, or by what criteria, is unclear.

Appointment tension

Formal independence coexists with consequential appointment/approval powers held elsewhere.

Financial-dependence tension

Formal independence coexists with high dependence on one public funder.

Strategy / allocation mismatch

Observed allocation materially differs from stated priority; requires like-for-like time and programme comparison.

Input / output mismatch

Resource change and measured output move differently; no causal inference without further evidence.

Geographic mismatch

Stated geographic redistribution and observed allocation differ.

Institutional mismatch

Partnership/ecosystem language coexists with concentrated formal authority/resources.

Time mismatch

The strategy describes future machinery not yet implemented.

Insufficient evidence

Available evidence does not support a meaningful comparison.
